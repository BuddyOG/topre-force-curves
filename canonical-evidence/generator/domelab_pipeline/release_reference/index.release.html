Method

Press and return strokes are recorded at 0.005 mm displacement steps on a calibrated bench. intake-qc-v1.4 is the retention authority for this review build, including integrity, target-speed-cohort, and replicate-agreement gates. Its retained-cohort RAMP repeatability check is advisory only: “ACCEPT + RAMP REVIEW” never changes retention or metric values. Scalar landmarks/markers are arithmetic means of per-run detections calculated at full precision. Averaged visualization traces are built separately and are not re-analyzed to create those scalar landmarks/markers; a marker therefore need not lie on a feature of the averaged trace. Rounding is presentation only.

Collapse is the tactile peak of the press curve. Valley is the post-collapse minimum. Snap % = (Collapse − Valley) / Collapse × 100 — the dome-switch industry's tactile-ratio standard, where linear = 0% and higher is crisper. Travel is detected where the bottom-out wall begins — the first point past the valley where force rises by more than 1% of its current value per 0.005 mm step, sustained for three consecutive steps. Full-stroke press work is the area under the press curve from 0 to Travel, in gf·mm — the total work your finger does across the keystroke. Pre-collapse work is the same integral taken from 0 to Collapse — the closest energy proxy for how hard the dome feels to overcome. Drop = Collapse − Valley in gf; Drop rate divides it by the collapse-to-valley distance; Normalized drop rate divides the snap fraction instead — peak-relative sharpness per mm. Steepest 0.10 mm drop is the fastest force loss over any 0.10 mm window between Collapse and Valley. Ramp is the pre-collapse buildup over the baseline-relative 10–90% rise (seating transient excluded). Work converts to SI as 1 gf·mm = 9.80665 µJ. These quantities describe different aspects of the stroke and are not a single universal measure of feel.

Assembly scope. Detected force-wall onset is an operational proxy from the complete measured test assembly. It is not nominal dome height, physical switch travel, actuation travel, or a universal contact position. Recorded turnaround range (test limit) is the minimum–maximum of the maximum press displacement captured across retained runs (one exact value when identical). It describes acquisition extent only and is never physical travel. If no force-wall meets the method, the force-wall and press-work-to-force-wall fields remain “Not detected”; a turnaround is never substituted.
